What Good Looks Like

Patterns from organizations that can answer the CFO's question

Drawn from leading enterprises across financial services, technology, and operations-intensive sectors — organizations that have moved from faith-based AI spending to evidence-based portfolio steering.

The Value Taxonomy: A Shared Language First
Without a taxonomy, every downstream artifact — prioritization, measurement, funding — rests on undefined terms

The first structural move is building an enterprise-wide classification framework: Vertical investments (direct P&L, owned by a business unit) vs. Horizontal investments (enabling capability, owned by the CoE). This single distinction ends the false comparison between infrastructure costs and business returns — and makes the CFO conversation structurally possible. Organizations that skip this step never achieve consistent ROI measurement.

Step-Zero Baselining: The Most Skipped Step
45% faster ROI realization in firms that document process state before every deployment

The proven approach: before any AI deployment, capture an exhaustive snapshot of the target workflow — cycle times, error rates, cost per transaction, exception rates. This "deterministic before" state is the foundation of every future ROI calculation. Organizations that skip this step are unable to prove value even when AI is clearly working. The baseline must be established before capital is allocated, not reconstructed after the fact.

Hub-and-Spoke CoE: Governance That Enables
2.5× faster time-to-value · 70% fewer IT escalations with a Hub-and-Spoke operating model

The model that works: a central AI governance office (3–8 people) sets the value taxonomy, intake gates, and Responsible AI standards. Named Business Unit AI Leads own day-to-day execution within those guardrails. Stage-gate approvals run in under 72 hours. The CoE treats governance as an accelerant, not a checkpoint — every approved initiative starts with a predefined value hypothesis and a step-zero baseline already in place.

Live Portfolio Dashboard: Faith-Based AI Ends Here
Single source of truth for the Portfolio Steering Committee — programmatic, live, tied to the general ledger

The organizations that have solved the CFO question run a live dashboard that traces every dollar of verified Vertical value, attributes Horizontal platform cost back through downstream use cases, and surfaces drift, adoption, and cost telemetry in real time. The attribution formula — Horizontal Cost Recovered = Σ(Vertical_value × Platform_utilization_share) — solves the enterprise technology headache of distributing shared platform costs across individual business unit P&L returns.

The Measurement Gap: Why AI ROI Is Structurally Hard
Challenge

74% of companies struggle to achieve and scale value.1 The problem is architectural. Most organizations deploy AI before establishing baselines, classifying investment types, or wiring telemetry to the general ledger. ROI becomes unmeasurable by design.

How it's being addressed

Leading organizations establish "deterministic scaffolding" before any deployment: data context (AI-ready enterprise data), workflow baselines (the "before" state), and control logic (telemetry, human-in-the-loop governance, automated variance checks in production).

Best Practices
No funding without a certified "deterministic before" baseline for the target workflow
Value taxonomy built first — Vertical vs. Horizontal classification before any prioritization
Step-zero maturity assessment to expose actual gaps between AI spend and P&L impact
Experimentation Drain: Shadow AI & Fragmented Pilots
Challenge

Only 12% of organizations are "Transformers" capturing continuous AI value. The remaining 88% are stuck in experimentation drain — fragmented, unscaled pilots duplicating tools, fragmenting data, and bypassing governance. Shadow AI creates risk that compounds quietly while ROI remains unprovable.

How it's being addressed

Audit before policy — forensic review of all active and hidden pilots before governance frameworks are written. Vertical/Horizontal classification ends the false comparison between infrastructure spend and business returns. Intake gates require a value hypothesis before any capital is allocated.

Best Practices
Inventory of experimentation: map all pilots before building governance
Classify every initiative as Vertical (P&L, BU-owned) or Horizontal (enabling, CoE-owned)
Kill low-potential initiatives early via mandatory intake gates and prioritization scorecards
AI amnesty programs to surface undeclared deployments without penalty
Operating Model: The CoE That Enables Rather Than Blocks
Challenge

Most AI governance failures trace to organizational causes — unclear ownership, absent accountability, no financial responsibility once a pilot goes to production. Traditional IT ownership models don't cover business-built AI. Strategy is outpacing infrastructure: 42% of executives feel strategy-ready, but data, tech, and talent capabilities lag.

How it's being addressed

Hub-and-Spoke CoE: central office (3–8 people) sets taxonomy, intake gates, and Responsible AI standards. Named Business Unit AI Leads own day-to-day execution within guardrails. Approval SLAs under 72 hours. Governance treated as an accelerant, not a gatekeeper.

Best Practices
Named owner and value hypothesis required for every initiative before deployment
Stage-gate approvals by risk and value tier — fast lanes for low-complexity, compliant use cases
Cross-functional governance committee with defined escalation paths
Financial accountability model: who owns the ROI claim once a pilot goes live?

The Common AI Platform: Built Once, Reused Everywhere
Challenge

Enterprises spend 93% of their AI budget on technology — yet duplicate the same scaffolding across every pilot. Each new initiative rebuilds LLM gateways, vector databases, and MLOps pipelines from scratch. The result: compounding technical debt, rising cost-per-initiative, and a horizontal cost base that finance cannot attribute to business returns.

How it's being addressed

A Common AI Platform separates Horizontal infrastructure (built once by the CoE) from Vertical use cases (built on top by business units). Six shared layers — infrastructure, data foundation, AI services/gateway, models, orchestration, experiences — wrapped in cross-cutting governance and observability. Built once. Reused everywhere.

Best Practices
Architecture before tooling — define the reference model before selecting vendors
Data orchestration standards: quality SLAs and ingestion protocols that turn messy records into AI-ready assets
Attribution formula: Horizontal costs flow back through downstream Vertical returns
Talent strategy: map skills gaps before deploying the platform — AI Value Engineers, Prompt Engineers, Data Stewards
The Measurement Action Loop: From Pilot to Proof
Challenge

88% of Fortune 500 enterprises are stranded at maturity Levels 1 or 2 — burning capital on vanity metrics and manual post-launch reviews without ever linking outputs back to the general ledger. When ROI is measured at all, it is measured after the fact, through estimation rather than telemetry, and against no documented baseline.

How it's being addressed

The Measurement Action Loop: Workflow Baselining (step-zero snapshot before AI) → Instrumented Pilot Execution (ring-fenced deployment with live telemetry) → Value Realization Audit (quantitative comparison against the baseline) → Live Portfolio ROI Dashboard (real-time, general ledger-tied). Each step produces evidence the next requires.

Best Practices
Baseline metrics: cycle times, error rates, cost per transaction, exception rates — captured before any AI deployment
Live telemetry wired from pilot day one — not retrofitted after launch
Value Realization Audit: strict quantitative evaluation comparing live telemetry against the deterministic baseline
Portfolio dashboard solves the attribution problem: distributes horizontal platform costs across individual BU P&L returns
