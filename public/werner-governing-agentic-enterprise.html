Context

Traditional governance was built for AI that advises.
Agentic AI acts.

The Prior Era
  • AI tools that assist and advise humans
  • Static models with predictable outputs
  • Narrow, purpose-built applications
  • Human review before any action is taken
  • Governed by data quality and bias audits
The Agentic Era
  • AI agents that plan, decide, and take action
  • Dynamic behavior across complex workflows
  • Agents calling APIs and triggering transactions
  • Action at machine speed — before human review
  • Multi-agent chains operating outside IT visibility
42% of organizations are already running agents in production — governance hasn't kept pace. The frameworks built for the prior era were not designed for AI that takes action before human review.

The response that works: Discover → Classify → Channel
Discover

Map what's actually deployed before writing policies. CASBs, network monitoring, AI gateways. Governance built on incomplete inventory always fails.

Classify

Tier AI tools: fully approved / conditionally approved (specific use cases + guardrails) / prohibited. Simple, enforceable, consistent.

Channel

Replace prohibition with sanctioned alternatives that meet the speed demands driving shadow behavior. Bans alone fail — behavior re-emerges in new tools.

Governance Landscape

Five capabilities that separate organizations that govern well from those that don't

All five are relevant to Werner. The next section shows what each looks like when done well.

1 Foundation Governance Framework Design: Risk classification taxonomy, ownership model, approval criteria, and policy architecture — before tooling selection. Reversing this sequence is the most common and costly mistake.
2 Urgent Shadow AI Discovery & Management: Structured audit of current tool usage, classification, and a remediation roadmap. Must happen before policies are written — policy without inventory is unenforceable.
3 Foundation Operating Model & Accountability: AI governance committee, business unit AI leads, named ownership for every agent. Unclear accountability compounds every other governance failure.
4 Enablement Citizen Development Enablement: Sanctioned platforms with built-in guardrails that channel innovation rather than suppress it. Approved catalogs, training requirements, and lightweight oversight mechanisms.
5 Scale At-Scale Governance Infrastructure: Agent registry, control plane, behavioral monitoring, automated compliance reporting. Human review at agent volume is unsustainable — automation is the only viable path.
What Good Looks Like

Patterns from organizations ahead of the curve

Drawn from transportation, logistics, and large operations-intensive enterprises — most analogous to Werner's scale and complexity.

Shadow AI: Discovery Before Policy
3–5× more tools found than expected — logistics orgs average 200–400 on first audit

The proven sequence: network monitoring + CASB + manager interviews + vendor spend analysis. Two cross-functional sprints. Risk-tiered catalog by week 6. Policy rollout by week 8. Governance built on incomplete inventory always fails.

Hub-and-Spoke Operating Model
2.5× faster decisions · 70% fewer IT escalations with named BU AI Leads

Central AI governance office (3–8 people) sets taxonomy and policy. Named Business Unit AI Leads own day-to-day governance in their domain. Monthly scorecard to leadership. Approval SLAs under 72 hours. This model treats governance as a business function, not a compliance checkbox.

Citizen Development Done Right
40–60% drop in shadow AI incidents within 6 months of launching a governed platform

Channel demand rather than block it. Copilot Studio, Power Automate, and ServiceNow Flow are the dominant enterprise citizen platforms — all with native DLP controls. Organizations with a governed platform saw incidents drop within two quarters. The governed path must be faster than the workaround.

The Agent Registry: Start Simple
Compliance audit time cut from days to hours — orgs with registries report 70% faster reviews

Start with a spreadsheet: owner, use case, data accessed, approval status, last review. Purpose-built tooling (Credo AI, IBM OpenPages, ServiceNow CMDB) scales later when volume demands it. What mattered first was the discipline of knowing what's deployed.

Governance Frameworks for Agentic AI
Challenge

42% of orgs run agents in production — governance hasn't kept pace. Frameworks built for static, advisory AI don't govern agents that act before human review. The gap is structural, not incremental.

How it's being addressed

NIST AI RMF (US default, risk-proportionate), EU AI Act (€35M fines, enforcing now), ISO 42001 management standard. Singapore IMDA 2026 is the first framework purpose-built for autonomous agents.

Best Practices
Tiered risk classification — low-risk moves fast, high-stakes gets proportionate scrutiny
Governance embedded in CI/CD pipelines, not policy docs alone
Defined autonomy limits and human-in-the-loop triggers documented before deployment
Shadow AI & Citizen Development
Challenge

40–65% of employees use unapproved AI tools; fewer than 20% believe it's wrong. Citizen-built agents access production systems and automate workflows entirely outside IT visibility.

How it's being addressed

Discover → Classify → Channel. Detection-first via CASB, AI gateway, and DLP. Three-tier classification: approved / conditional / prohibited. Channel via sanctioned platforms — prohibition alone consistently fails.

Best Practices
Discovery before policy — CASB and network monitoring before writing any rules
Sanctioned citizen dev platforms: Copilot Studio, Power Platform, ServiceNow
AI amnesty programs to surface undeclared deployments without penalty
Mandatory AI literacy training to shift culture, not just control
Controls, Approvals & Ownership Models
Challenge

Most AI failures trace to organizational causes — unclear ownership, absent approvals, no accountability. Traditional IT ownership models don't cover business-built agents.

How it's being addressed

Three governance roles: AI Executive Sponsor (CIO, cross-org accountability), Model Owner (BU leader, agent risk and performance), AI Champion (BU operational, day-to-day governance). Stage-gate approvals by risk tier.

Best Practices
Named owner required for every agent before deployment
Stage-gate approvals: self-certification (low-risk) through full legal review (high-risk)
Cross-functional governance committee, bi-weekly cadence
Regular third-party AI audits as accountability mechanism

Balancing Innovation with Risk & Compliance
Challenge

Development teams see governance as friction; compliance teams see ungoverned AI as existential risk. EU Act now enforcing (€35M fines) while the US remains permissive — adding real complexity for any organization operating across both environments.

How it's being addressed

Governance as enablement, not gatekeeping. Pre-approved tool catalogs and green lanes for low-risk use cases remove friction for compliant work. Federated model: central policy ownership, distributed business-unit execution.

Best Practices
Pre-approved AI catalogs — overhead only for non-catalog work
CI/CD policy gates — compliant deployments move at full speed
Autonomy trust ladder: extend agent autonomy incrementally as performance data builds confidence
Unified access governance for humans and AI agents under one IAM framework
Governing Agent Development at Scale
Challenge

Large enterprises now manage hundreds to thousands of active agents — manual governance cannot keep pace. 97% of organizations experiencing AI-related breaches lacked adequate security controls. Standard frameworks weren't designed for agents that continuously evolve.

How it's being addressed

Agent registry as system of record (owner, risk tier, data access, approval status). Real-time policy enforcement layer: behavioral monitoring, prompt guardrails, automated compliance reporting. Unified AI control plane across all systems and teams.

Best Practices
Architecture before tooling — reverse order is the most common failure mode
Automated evidence generation as byproduct of operations — not a separate documentation exercise
Every agent as a managed identity in IAM: credentials, permissions, audit trails
System-level governance for multi-agent workflows — orchestration rules defined explicitly before deployment
