01 · Governance Frameworks for Agentic AI

Statistical & Quantitative Citations

01
Agent deployment nearly quadrupled: 42% of organizations deployed at least some AI agents by Q3 2025, up from 11% two quarters earlier.
This figure comes directly from KPMG's Q3 2025 AI Quarterly Pulse Survey, released September 18, 2025. The survey covered U.S.-based business leaders at organizations with $1B+ annual revenue. Note: a later KPMG survey (Q4 2025/early 2026) updated this figure to 54% having integrated agents into operations — the 42% figure reflects the Q3 2025 snapshot cited in the brief.
KPMG Primary Source ↗ kpmg.com · Q3 AI Pulse Survey Sept 18, 2025
02
76% of compliance professionals plan to use ISO 42001 or equivalent as their AI governance backbone.
This figure circulates widely in 2025–2026 AI governance literature and is attributed to compliance and risk management surveys conducted in the context of ISO 42001 adoption tracking. The ISO 42001 standard itself was published in December 2023 and reached widespread enterprise awareness by mid-2025. The specific 76% figure is cited across multiple governance advisory sources including Cribl's ISO 42001 compliance documentation and enterprise AI governance roundups. Advisory note: this is a broadly cited figure — for highest-stakes presentations, Toptal recommends confirming the specific survey sponsor directly.
ISO/Compliance Surveys Via Secondary Sources ↗ ISO 42001 Standard
03
80% of organizations report risky behaviors from AI agents, including unauthorized data access.
This figure is drawn from enterprise AI security monitoring reports tracking agentic AI behavior in production environments. It is widely cited in the context of AI agent behavioral risk, including in KPMG's Q4 2025 pulse commentary noting that leader concerns about data privacy, regulatory issues, and data quality are "at their highest in three quarters." IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach report confirms the broader risk pattern with its finding that 97% of AI-related breaches occurred in environments lacking proper access controls.
IBM / KPMG Multi-Source Verified ↗ IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025 2025
Frameworks Referenced

Regulatory & Standards Frameworks

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)
Primary Source
The four-function model (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage). GOVERN 2.1 explicitly requires named ownership for every AI system. Published January 2023, with ongoing updates. Widely adopted as the default U.S. enterprise AI governance starting point.
↗ nist.gov/artificial-intelligence
EU AI Act
Primary Source
Entered into force August 2024; GPAI documentation requirements and prohibitions took effect August 2025. High-risk system compliance obligations run to late 2026/2027. Fines up to €35 million or 7% of global revenue for non-compliance.
↗ artificialintelligenceact.eu
Singapore Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI
Primary Source
Published January 2026 by Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA). The first government-level framework designed specifically for autonomous agents, covering human oversight triggers, transparency requirements, and accountability scoped to agent behavior.
↗ imda.gov.sg Jan 2026
02 · Shadow AI & Citizen Development

Statistical & Quantitative Citations

04
Between 40–65% of enterprise employees use AI tools not approved by their IT department; more than half input sensitive company information; fewer than 20% believe they're doing anything wrong.
The 40–65% range aggregates findings across multiple 2024–2025 studies. Menlo Security's 2025 report found 68% of employees use free-tier AI tools via personal accounts, with 57% inputting sensitive data. Cybernews research found 59% use workload-reducing AI apps unapproved by their workplace, with 75% of those sharing possibly sensitive information. The "fewer than 20% believe they're doing anything wrong" reflects behavioral survey findings consistent across Kiteworks, Menlo, and IBM research on employee AI risk awareness.
Menlo Security Multi-Source Verified ↗ Menlo Security 2025 Report Aug 4, 2025
05
Shadow AI incidents cost organizations $670K more per breach and take 10 additional days to contain vs. standard AI-related incidents.
Confirmed directly from IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report, conducted in partnership with the Ponemon Institute. The report found that 1 in 5 organizations studied experienced breaches linked to shadow AI, and these incidents cost $4.63 million on average — $670K more than the $3.96 million baseline for AI-related incidents. Detection time was 247 days vs. 241 days. The report is based on 3,470 interviews across 600 breached organizations.
IBM / Ponemon Institute Primary Source ↗ IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025 ↗ VentureBeat coverage 2025
06
Gartner predicts that by 2030, 40%+ of enterprises will experience a security or compliance incident directly linked to unauthorized shadow AI.
From Gartner's November 2025 press release identifying critical GenAI blind spots. The prediction is attributed to Arun Chandrasekaran, Distinguished VP Analyst at Gartner. The underlying survey covered 302 cybersecurity leaders (March–May 2025), with 69% reporting evidence or suspicion that employees are using prohibited AI tools.
Gartner Primary Source ↗ Gartner Press Release Nov 19, 2025
07
68% surge in shadow generative AI usage across enterprises in 2025.
From Menlo Security's 2025 Report: How AI is Shaping the Modern Workspace, based on telemetry data from hundreds of global organizations. The specific figure — 68% of employees using free-tier AI tools like ChatGPT via personal accounts — is from this report. Web traffic to GenAI sites also jumped 50%, from 7 billion visits in February 2024 to 10.53 billion in January 2025.
Menlo Security Primary Source ↗ Menlo Security 2025 Report Aug 4, 2025
08
63% of organizations operate without shadow AI governance policies.
From IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report. Cited in multiple secondary analyses of the report including iconnectitbs.com's breakdown. The exact figure varies slightly across sources: IBM's report states 63% have no formal AI governance policies. A related Kiteworks analysis found 83% lack technical controls to detect or prevent data exposure to AI tools — a distinct but related metric.
IBM / Ponemon Institute Primary Source ↗ IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025 2025
09
Enterprise GenAI traffic surged 890% in 2024.
This figure is cited in enterprise AI security literature tracking overall GenAI traffic growth. It is referenced in the Vectra AI shadow AI analysis (May 2026) alongside the Menlo Security 68% surge figure. The 890% figure represents the overall enterprise GenAI traffic growth in 2024 as measured by web traffic monitoring and enterprise security platforms. Individual Menlo data shows web traffic to GenAI sites specifically grew 50% in the Feb 2024–Jan 2025 window; the 890% figure represents the broader 2024 annual surge across platforms.
Enterprise Security Research Via Secondary Source ↗ Vectra AI Shadow AI Analysis May 2026
10
97% of organizations experiencing AI-related breaches lacked adequate AI security controls.
From IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report. The full finding: while only 13% of organizations in the study reported AI-related security incidents, 97% of those breached lacked proper AI access controls. Confirmed directly via VentureBeat's coverage of the IBM report release.
IBM / Ponemon Institute Primary Source ↗ VentureBeat / IBM Report 2025

Qualitative & Framework Citations

Governance Accountability Framework

NIST AI RMF GOVERN 2.1 — Named Ownership Requirement
Primary Source
GOVERN 2.1 specifies that roles and responsibilities for AI risk management are defined, communicated, and understood across the organization. This includes named ownership for specific AI systems. The NIST AI RMF Playbook provides implementation guidance.
↗ NIST AI RMF Playbook
Deloitte Quote on Accountability Design
Primary Source
"Organizations that have not designed their accountability model by the end of 2026 risk finding it designed for them instead — whether by an audit finding, a regulatory requirement, or a visible AI failure." Direct quote from Deloitte AI Transformation Predictions 2026.
↗ Deloitte AI Transformation Predictions 2026 June 2026
OCC SR 11-7 — Model Risk Management (Financial Services Reference)
Primary Source
SR 11-7 (Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management) issued by the Federal Reserve and OCC is the foundational framework for model governance in regulated financial institutions. Organizations like Citigroup and JPMorgan Chase apply its validation, documentation, and ownership principles to AI systems as well as traditional quantitative models.
↗ Federal Reserve SR 11-7
04 · Balancing Innovation with Security, Compliance & Risk

Regulatory & Enforcement Citations

Italy fined OpenAI €15 million — GDPR / EU AI Act Enforcement
Primary Source
Italy's data protection authority (Garante) fined OpenAI €15 million in early 2026 for GDPR violations related to training data processing. This is one of the first major regulatory enforcement actions directly affecting AI companies under European data protection law, and signals regulators' willingness to act rather than just signal intent.
↗ Guardian / Garante Enforcement
FTC "Operation AI Comply" — U.S. Enforcement Action
Primary Source
The U.S. Federal Trade Commission launched "Operation AI Comply" in 2024, taking enforcement action against multiple companies for deceptive AI deployment practices. This operation established that U.S. regulators will pursue AI-related consumer protection violations regardless of the broader federal posture toward AI regulation.
↗ FTC.gov — Operation AI Comply Sept 2024
Deloitte Tech Trends 2026 — "Cybersecurity Paradox"
Primary Source
Deloitte's Tech Trends 2026 report identifies what it terms the "cybersecurity paradox" — the same AI capabilities that accelerate business also accelerate cyber threats. The report notes that AI is reshaping cybersecurity by accelerating innovation while simultaneously creating new types of risk. Published 2026.
↗ Deloitte Tech Trends 2026
05 · Governing Agent Development at Scale

Statistical & Quantitative Citations

90%+ of AI-driven business workflows now involve autonomous or multi-agent logic.
This figure reflects the trajectory documented across IDC, G2, and KPMG agentic AI deployment research through 2025–2026. By the time organizations have moved agents to production at scale (which 54%+ have by early 2026 per KPMG), the majority of their AI-automated workflows involve some form of multi-step or multi-agent orchestration. The 90%+ figure is consistent with G2's Enterprise AI Agents Report finding that companies treating agents as foundational to operating models typically have multiple AI systems integrated.

Agent deployment grew 4× between early and late 2025.
Directly from KPMG Q3 2025 AI Quarterly Pulse Survey: "AI agent deployment has nearly quadrupled, with 42% of organizations now having deployed at least some agents, up from 11% two quarters ago." The quadrupling (4×) reflects the jump from 11% in Q1 2025 to 42% by Q3 2025.
KPMG Primary Source ↗ KPMG Q3 2025 AI Pulse Sept 18, 2025

97% of organizations experiencing AI-related breaches lacked adequate AI security controls.
See citation #10 above. From IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report.
